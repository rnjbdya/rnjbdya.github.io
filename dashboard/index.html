The research program

Four questions, one controlled theme

Rotation is both a nuisance and a source of structure. These experiments ask where pose information lives, when it hurts recognition, and whether the desired transformation law can be learned—or should be built in.

Controlled protocol

Rotate first. Let each method be itself afterward.

This is the protocol for the unified cross-method benchmark. The retrospective encoder sweeps and synthetic SO(2) studies state their own splits and controls in their result cards. For the benchmark, the experimental transform stays outside the architecture, preserving a shared task while allowing author-specific sampling, neighborhoods, features, and optimization.

Deterministic evaluation

Evaluation rotations are derived from experiment seed, split, and stable sample identity. Coordinates and declared vector attributes receive the same transform.

Honest provenance

Author-native integrations are distinguished from controlled adaptations. Smoke tests are labeled as software validation rather than scientific evidence.

Reading the numbers

Similar metrics answer different questions

Overall accuracy

Fraction of correctly classified objects. Higher is better.

Cosine similarity

Directional agreement with the canonical embedding. High cosine alone does not prove invariance.

Relative displacement

Feature movement normalized by canonical feature norm. Lower means less motion.

Orbit variance

Share of observed variation associated with rotation within an object. It does not specify a transformation law.

Action error

How well a fitted latent transformation predicts a held-out rotated state. Lower is better.

Composition error

Whether two fitted rotations combine like their geometric counterparts. Lower is better.
